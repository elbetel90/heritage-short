# Design

## Audience

The film is for people about 16 to 35 who already feel the absurd in the sense of Camus's *The Stranger* and *The Myth of Sisyphus*. They ask what a life is for, and the world returns no answer. That silence is the experience the film is about.

They watch on a phone. The primary frame is vertical, 9:16. The 16:9 export is the same film for a screen that is not a phone.

## Intent

A viewer should leave holding two things at once. The absurd is real, and it is not removed by looking away. What remains is a small light inside this life: accept the life they actually have, stay lucid, keep going, and care for a person they love.

Diversion is any habit that turns the mind off the silence without answering it. Religion used as a leap, a total scientific or political explanation, substance use, status, a career that is supposed to make a person legitimate, a romance treated as the missing meaning, scrolling and noise, shopping and self-improvement, "everything happens for a reason," waiting for a worthier life, and cynicism that refuses to care are all the same move. This film uses one of them: late-night self-improvement podcasts, habit streaks, and checklists. It does not treat that habit as an answer.

The ending is not a solved crisis. The silence is still there. The character knows the morning will not thank him, and he does the small human thing anyway. That act is the light. Hope in this film is tea left for someone who is asleep. It is not a rescue from outside this life.

## Story

Tarek counts his nights so they will finally mean something. The podcast ends and the street does not. He leaves tea outside his sleeping sister's door, knowing the morning will not thank him.

Tarek is fictional, in his early twenties, and shares a small apartment with his younger sister, Nadia. She is fictional too. She never appears and she never thanks him. The door stays closed. Neither face is a real person's likeness. The consent record is in `docs/ETHICS.md`.

Narration is English, in short concrete lines. The word "absurd" is never spoken. The burned-in subtitles are the same English lines. Shots 05 and 06 are room tone only, so the silence can be heard. Subjects sit in the center of the 9:16 frame so the 16:9 crop still holds them. The film does not end on a smile, a sunrise that redeems him, or Nadia opening the door.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Diversion | Close-up of Tarek's thumb on a habit tracker in a dim room. The streak count is visible. | "Forty-one days. The app said the streak was who I was becoming." | He is trying to become someone whose counted days add up. |
| 02 | Diversion | Medium shot of Tarek in earbuds, pacing a narrow hallway. | "A man in my ears told me the morning was mine if I took it before the world did." | The talk promises that the morning can be owned. |
| 03 | Diversion | A timer counting down, and water filled exactly to a line on a glass. | "Water to the line. Two minutes. Then I would be ready to live." | Living has been turned into a measurement. |
| 04 | Silence | The episode ends while the voice is still promising. An earbud hangs. The fridge hums. A bus passes outside. | "He was still talking when the episode ended. The fridge kept going." | The world continues the moment without him. |
| 05 | Silence | Tarek stands still at the kitchen counter, hands empty. No screen in his hands. | None. Room tone only. | The want for a reason is left with nothing to do. |
| 06 | Silence | Grey early light through a window. A clock reads the hour of Nadia's shift. The same bus sound, farther off. | None. Room tone only. | The clock is no longer his tool. Her shift will come anyway. |
| 07 | Turn | He looks from his empty hands to her closed door down the hall. No light under the door. | "Her shift starts whether I understand my life or not." | What he can do is smaller than what he feels. |
| 08 | Turn | The phone is on the counter, the next episode waiting. His hand hesitates, then leaves it. He sets the timer and the checklist aside. | "I could put the earbud back. The next one was already waiting." | He refuses the diversion. Nothing is solved. |
| 09 | Light | The kettle heats and he pours tea into a plain mug. His face stays ordinary. | "The water boiled the way it boils on any other night." | The care is real, and the water does not care. |
| 10 | Light | He sets the mug on a mat outside Nadia's door. He does not knock. He stays in the quiet hall one moment longer, then the film ends on the closed door and the steam. | "She will drink it, or she will not. I left it there." | He continues without a witness and without a reward. |

## User tests

A user test is a showing, not a software check. Three people who are like the audience watch a cut. Their words are written here, and the storyboard changes because of what they said. Neither showing has happened yet.

### Radio cut

Not run yet. This is the first showing, and it happens before any keyframe. The cut is a black screen, Tarek's English narration, and a quiet placeholder score. Play it to at least three people about 16 to 35 who know this kind of restlessness. Ask whether the silence lands when the episode ends and the fridge keeps going, and whether the mug left for someone who does not open the door feels like a small hope or like a puzzle. Write what they said below, and change the storyboard before any Cloudflare or Kaggle spend.

### Near-final cut

Not run yet. This is the second showing, after pictures, the chosen score, and the burned-in subtitles exist. Play that cut to at least three people from the same audience, on a phone speaker. Write what they said below, and change the storyboard again. A line that was clear with only a voice can still fail once the habit tracker is on screen.
